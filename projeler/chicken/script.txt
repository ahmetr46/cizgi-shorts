Why do the rich get richer and the poor get poorer? Picture a starving village where everyone wakes up with the exact same single chicken.

Same starting line, same asset—so how do three totally different futures happen from one single hen?

Group one eats the chicken that night—great soup, zero tomorrow. Group two eats one egg a day: never starving, never growing. Group three gathers birds into a shared coop, returns part of the eggs to group two, and incubates the rest. Chicks hatch in 21 days, lay eggs in five to six months, and extra eggs buy feed. One coop becomes two, four, eight—snowballing fast. Group one protests unfairness in the square, until group three's booming coops hire them for three eggs a day.

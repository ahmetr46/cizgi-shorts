Even if your boss in Japan wants to fire you, chances are they can't. Mr. Tanaka has been at the same company for twenty years, and one morning the boss decides Tanaka has to go. But Japanese labor law is clear: without a just and reasonable cause, firing is invalid. Calling someone unproductive isn't enough most of the time. Even saying the company is downsizing isn't enough. So what does the company do? Instead of firing Tanaka, they bore him out. They put him in a completely empty room. There's a desk and a chair. No computer, no phone, no work. The Japanese call this oidashi beya. Basically, a firing room. From nine to five, Tanaka stares at the wall. Stares out the window, counts the hours. The goal is obvious: make him bored and ashamed so he resigns on his own. But there's another side to it. In Japan, resigning isn't easy either. The boss tears up the resignation letter. "What will the team do if you leave?" Because of that, people now pay to have someone resign for them. You hire an agency, they call your boss, and the best part is, in twenty twenty-four, an employee at a resignation agency wanted to quit. Guess what they did? They hired another resignation agency. Yeah.

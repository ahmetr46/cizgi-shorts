In the eighteen thirties, ketchup was sold as medicine. An Ohio doctor, John Cook Bennett, claimed tomatoes could cure diarrhea, indigestion, even jaundice. People laughed. Tomatoes? Medicine? He published ketchup recipes anyway. Then a salesman turned it into pills. Tomato pills! Rival brands popped up everywhere, each one promising miracles. But when people looked inside... some pills had no tomato at all. Just laxatives. By eighteen fifty, the craze was dead. And the medicine? It ended up on your fries.
